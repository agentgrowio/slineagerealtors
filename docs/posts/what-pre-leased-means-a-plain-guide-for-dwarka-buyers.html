What Pre-Leased Means: A Plain Guide for Dwarka Buyers

What “pre-leased” really means at The Omaxe State, Dwarka: what to read in Omaxe's agreement, and the questions to ask before you book a unit.

S Lineage Realtors · Nov 20, 2026 · 4 min read

“If the shop is pre-leased, is my rent guaranteed?”

It is usually the first thing a buyer asks me, and it is a fair question. The plain answer: “pre-leased” means the unit is sold with its leasing arranged as part of the deal. At The Omaxe State, the rental terms are as per Omaxe's agreement. What you receive, from when and for how long is written in that agreement, not in the word “pre-leased”.

So the real work is not decoding a label. It is reading one document carefully before you book. This guide explains what to look for, in the order I go through it with buyers.

What “pre-leased” means, in one paragraph

With an ordinary shop, you buy the space and then find a tenant yourself: agents, negotiations, a rent agreement, and the risk of months with nobody in it. With a pre-leased unit, the leasing is arranged as part of the purchase, so you are buying a property and an income arrangement together. That is why it appeals to people who do not want to hunt for tenants, or who live in Gurugram, another city or another country.

The arrangement is only as good as its written terms. Two pre-leased units can look identical on a brochure and read very differently in the agreement.

If a figure matters to you, ask where it is written.

Pre-leased is not the same as guaranteed

A lease is an agreement with terms: when it starts, how long it runs, how the rent changes over time, who pays which charges, and what happens if the tenant leaves. “Pre-leased” on its own answers none of these questions. The agreement answers all of them.

That is why I never quote a rent figure or a percentage. A number said out loud in a sales conversation is worth nothing if the agreement says something else. I would rather put the agreement in front of you and go through it clause by clause, until every line makes sense to you.

What to read in Omaxe's agreement

These are the six things I go through with every buyer. Write the answers down as you read.

  1. When the rental arrangement starts. From which event or date does it apply to your unit?
  2. How long it runs. What is the term, and what happens when it ends?
  3. How the rent changes. Does it change over the term, and on what basis?
  4. Who pays which charges. Maintenance, common area charges and utilities: whose cost is each one?
  5. What happens if the space falls vacant. If a tenant leaves early, what does the agreement say happens next?
  6. Who you deal with. Who pays you, how, and whom do you contact if there is a problem?

If the agreement does not answer one of these clearly, ask before you book, and get the answer in writing.

Questions to bring to the site visit

A site visit is not only about seeing the location. Bring this list and tick it off:

  • Is the unit a retail shop, a food court unit or an office unit, and why does that type suit what I want?
  • Where exactly is it in the project: which district, which floor?
  • Can I see the current official cost sheet for this exact unit and payment plan?
  • Can I read the rental terms in Omaxe's agreement, in writing, before I book?
  • Does the RERA registration DLRERA2024P0003 on rera.delhi.gov.in match the project and promoter I am being shown?
  • Who can advise me on a loan or on tax? I can connect you with the right professional; I do not give that advice myself.

Who a pre-leased unit can suit

Salaried professionals. If you work in IT or a corporate job in Dwarka, West Delhi or Gurugram and want income beside your salary without becoming a landlord, the appeal is obvious. Read the agreement as carefully as you would read an offer letter.

People nearing or in retirement. If a steady monthly income matters most, take your time. Read the agreement with your family, and ask every question twice if you need to.

NRIs with Delhi roots. I can do the site walkthrough with you on a video call and handle the paperwork here in Delhi, from booking to the lease agreement. For repatriation and tax questions, I will connect you with the right professional.

Small business owners. If you want a shop to run yourself rather than rental income, tell me at the start. It is a different conversation, and I will tell you plainly what fits.
